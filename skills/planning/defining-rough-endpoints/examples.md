## Example

### Input shape (Pass 1)

```
Seed: A woman kneels in a throne room before a court assembled to witness her execution. The king who ordered her execution is her son. [Full seed document including genre: Fantasy / Political Fantasy / Dark Fantasy; themes: legitimacy and the cost of inheritance (interdependent with) the gap between what we make and what we become]
```

### Pass 1 Output (abbreviated)

```
Prologue specification:
  Warranted: YES
  Temporal relationship: 11 years before Chapter 1.
  POV/register: Third person limited — the mother, before she became the Dowager, on the night she decided to make her son king rather than claim the throne herself.
  What it establishes: The reader must enter Chapter 1 knowing that the mother had a choice, that she chose the son, and that she knew what it would cost. This cannot be established from within Chapter 1 without becoming exposition.
  What it withholds: Whether she regrets it. Whether she was right.
  Relationship to milestones: The prologue event is a precondition — it is the choice that makes Milestone 1 possible.

Starting situation:
The kingdom has been at peace for eleven years — the kind maintained through pressure rather than settlement. The king is young, effective, and aware that every faction supported him because he was the alternative to a worse option. His mother, under house arrest on charges that are technically true and politically necessary, is the instability that cannot remain as it is.

Draft ending destination:
The question of what legitimacy costs — and who pays it — will have been answered. Something will have been lost that cannot be recovered; the story ends in that loss's aftermath.

Draft milestones:
  1. The execution is carried out or not — the son makes the choice that determines who he is.
     Character slot A: The son / the king. [See slot sketch below]
  2. A crisis in which the political machinery the mother built begins working against the son.
     Character slot A (same): The son. The consequence structure of the mother's choices arrives.
  3. A moment in which the mother's full account of what she did — and why — is rendered.
     Character slot B: The mother. [See slot sketch below]
  4. [Held open — may or may not exist: a moment of genuine contact between them after everything.]

Character slot sketches:

Slot A — The King:
  Narrative role: The person whose choice at Milestone 1 defines the story's central question.
  Implied contradiction: Was made into something by someone who loved him / has become something that may have no use for love.
  Stakes: His legitimacy, his self-narrative, his capacity to be someone worth surviving as.
  World context: Young king, eleven years of regency shaped by his mother, now operating in personal rule; every faction's loyalty is contingent.
  Open questions:
    1. Does he believe the execution is necessary, or does he need to believe it?
    2. What would it mean for him to refuse — not politically, but psychologically?
    3. What does he think his mother actually wants from him in this moment?

Slot B — The Mother:
  Narrative role: The person whose full account of her choices reframes the story's events.
  Implied contradiction: Made the son king to protect him / or to extend herself / or both without being able to tell the difference.
  Stakes: What she is remembered as. Whether what she built survives her.
  World context: Former regent, now under arrest; the most politically intelligent person in the story, operating from a position of formal powerlessness.
  Open questions:
    1. What does she want in the final accounting — to be understood, to be forgiven, or something else entirely?
    2. When did she first know the execution was coming?
    3. What did she do in the years of the regency that she will not defend, even to herself?

Draft core conflict question:
Can a person survive becoming what someone who loved them made them, and still be someone worth surviving as?

Compass notes:
  The ending does not specify whether the son lives, dies, or is deposed.
  Milestone 1 is unsequenced — it may be the opening image, the midpoint, or the climax.
  Milestone 4 is held as a question; the story may refuse it, and that refusal may be the ending.
  The seed's questions about legitimacy as spiritual vs. political category remain open for Worldbuilding.
```
