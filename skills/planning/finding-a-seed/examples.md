## Example

### Input shape

```
Broad genre: Fantasy
Subgenres: Political Fantasy, Dark Fantasy
Primary themes:
  - "Legitimacy and the cost of inheritance"
  - "The gap between what we make and what we become"
  Interdependency: These two themes are interdependent — the seed must hold both in tension simultaneously.
Negative space: No chosen-one framing. No dark lord. Not a quest structure.
```

### Output shape (abbreviated)

```
─── DEFINITIVE SEED ───

Seed: A woman kneels in a throne room before a court assembled to witness her execution. She is the last legitimate heir to the throne. The king who ordered her execution is her son.

Method: Character-First combined with "What If?" — began with the impossible situation and asked what psychology could produce both figures without either being a monster.

Validation:
  Concreteness: PASS — specific room, specific moment, specific relationship; fully placeable.
  Generative Tension: PASS — raises more questions than it resolves; the history, the political necessity, and the human cost are all implied and none explained.
  Emotional Necessity: PASS — the mother/son structure makes the stake immediately and universally legible.
  Open Architecture: PASS — compatible with multiple narrative structures; no specific plot implied.

Genre and theme fit:
  Political Fantasy / Dark Fantasy: PASS — throne room, court, succession machinery are genre-native; darkness is human, not supernatural.
  Theme 1 (Legitimacy and inheritance): The execution is a legitimacy act — the son eliminates the prior claim to consolidate his own.
  Theme 2 (What we make / what we become): The mother made the king; the king is executing the maker. Both themes are structurally inseparable in this seed — interdependency holds.

Questions:
  1. What did the mother do — or fail to do — that made this moment her son's only viable choice?
  2. What does the son believe about himself in this moment, and how much of it is true?
  3. Is there a version of this world in which legitimacy is not a death sentence?
  4. Who is watching this execution, and what do they owe each of them?
  5. What is the mother thinking about — not her death, but what specifically?
  6. What did she want for him? What did she actually give him?
  7. Is "legitimate heir" a political category in this world or a spiritual one?
  8. What happens in the hour after the execution — to the court, to the son, to the body?
  9. Who in this room is lying about what they feel, and who is most dangerous because of it?
  10. [Not yet cleanly formulated: something about whether love and the making of rulers can coexist — whether you can make a king without making something that will eventually have no use for you.]

─── CANDIDATE SEEDS LOG ───

Candidate 1 — Vivid Image:
  Seed: A throne dragged to the centre of a great hall and set alight, the court watching in silence while the king who ordered it stands at the back in ordinary clothes.
  Strengths: Visually strong; the ordinary clothes carry theme weight; politically resonant.
  Failure point: Failed Open Architecture — implies an abdication or revolutionary moment with a narrow structural path. Insufficient generative tension for a novel.
  Status: Rejected.

Candidate 2 — "What If?":
  Seed: What if the only person who knows how to hold a fractured kingdom together is the person the new king had to destroy to take it?
  Strengths: Engages both interdependent themes; creates structural irony native to Political Fantasy.
  Failure point: Failed Concreteness — exists as a structural premise, not an image; cannot be placed with a camera.
  Status: Rejected. Note: strong enough to be considered as the core conflict question in Defining Rough Endpoints.

Candidate 3 — Historical Inspiration:
  Seed: Drew structural pattern from Eleanor of Aquitaine — a politically powerful woman who outlasts the system built to contain her, whose children become instruments of her containment.
  Strengths: Rich structural pattern; engages inheritance theme directly; strong emotional architecture.
  Failure point: Partial failure on Open Architecture — the Eleanor pattern implies a survival arc that narrows the story's options. Weaker than the definitive seed on Emotional Necessity (less concentrated).
  Status: Held as alternative. The "woman who outlasts her containment" pattern could anchor a viable alternative if the definitive seed is rejected.
```
