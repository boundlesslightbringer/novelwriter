## Example

### Input shape — Scene passing the audit

```
Scene draft: [Caradhen and Sethric ride out to inspect the northern pass. Sethric is quiet. Caradhen asks about the garrison readiness. Sethric gives precise, professional answers. Near the end of the ride, Caradhen says something about the envoy that is neutral on its face — that she is "competent." Sethric says nothing. Caradhen notices the silence. The scene ends with them returning to the hall without having said anything that mattered.]

Scene's intended function (from main agent): Quiet scene — establish that something is wrong between Caradhen and Sethric before the story makes it explicit.

Module 1.1 (Caradhen): [full record — core wound: plains battle; self-deception: believes Sethric has forgiven him]
Module 1.3 (Caradhen→Sethric): Valence: complex trust / unacknowledged guilt. Power: Caradhen holds formal authority; Sethric holds moral authority as witness. Trajectory: Stable surface / pressure fault underneath. Secrets: Caradhen conceals that he thinks about the plains often. Sethric conceals that he never stopped.
```

### Output shape — Passing verdict

```
Scene: Caradhen + Sethric, northern pass inspection. Caradhen POV.

Momentum verdict: EARNING ITS PLACE.

Conflict present — what is working:
  The scene is doing exactly what a quiet scene between these two characters should do. The conflict is entirely beneath the surface — it does not break through — and that is its structural point. What makes the scene work: Sethric's silence at the word "competent" is doing the work of an entire confrontation. Caradhen notices it. He does not name it. The reader holds both the silence and the knowledge of why it exists. This is interpersonal conflict operating at depth without a single line of overt tension. The internal conflict (Caradhen's self-deception about Sethric's forgiveness) is present in what Caradhen does not let himself think — the scene ends before he follows the silence to its conclusion. That incompletion is the scene's engine, not its weakness.

Conflict weight assessment:
  Internal: LATENT — present in what Caradhen does not let himself think. Appropriate for this scene's function.
  Interpersonal: ACTIVE at depth — Sethric's silence is the scene's primary event. Functioning at full weight.
  Societal: ABSENT — not applicable. The scene is private and the social codes of the clan are not relevant to what the scene is doing.
  Factional: ABSENT — not applicable. The scene is not about the alliance; it is about these two men.
  Existential: ABSENT — not applicable. The scene is building toward the existential question, not yet touching it.

Nothing materially missing.

Scene rhythm note: The scene's silence at the end is the right landing point. One consideration: the moment Caradhen says "competent" and Sethric goes quiet — if this is in the scene's final third, the reader may not have enough time to sit with it before the scene closes. If it is in the middle, the scene has room to breathe after the silence, which is better. Worth checking placement.
```

### Input shape — Scene failing the audit

```
Scene draft: [Aldric meets with his council to discuss the envoy's mission. Three councillors offer opinions. Aldric listens, thanks them, and dismisses them. He is described as "troubled by the political complexity." He decides to wait for Vara's report before acting.]

Scene's intended function: not provided.
Module 1.1 (Aldric): Emotional state: controlled, strategic. Active desire: consolidate authority. Active fear: being perceived as indecisive. Core contradiction: genuine belief in necessary ruthlessness / persistent need for external validation that he will not acknowledge.
```

### Output shape — Failing verdict

```
Scene: Aldric + council, discussion of the envoy's mission. Aldric POV.

Momentum verdict: UNDERLOADED.

Conflict present — what is working:
  The scene contains the structural elements of a council scene — a king receiving advice, weighing options, making a decision. The factional stakes are theoretically present (three councillors, presumably with different interests). The political situation is being discussed.

  What is not working: none of this is landing as conflict. The councillors are delivering information; Aldric is receiving it. The scene is an information transfer dressed as a deliberation. "Troubled by the political complexity" is declared conflict — the narration tells the reader that tension exists without creating its experience. The decision to wait is a non-decision that produces no consequence and advances nothing.

Conflict absent or underused:
  Internal (absent — available): Aldric's core contradiction — genuine belief in necessary ruthlessness / unacknowledged need for external validation — is the scene's most available engine and is entirely absent. A council scene is exactly where this contradiction should surface: the councillors are, structurally, the people whose opinion he tells himself he does not need. Does he trust their read of Caradhen? Does he notice that he wants to? Does the decision to "wait" feel like strategy or like relief that he does not have to act yet? His Module 1.1 record makes this scene's internal conflict fully available; the draft is not using it.

  Interpersonal (absent — available): The council scene has three councillors but no characters. No councillor is pursuing anything; no councillor has a position that conflicts with another's or with Aldric's. A council in which all advisors are functionally identical — delivering information, receiving thanks, being dismissed — is not a scene with interpersonal conflict. If even one councillor has a stake in the outcome (Module 1.3 records, if available, should be checked), that stake can carry the scene's interpersonal level without requiring any new plot material.

Conflict weight assessment:
  Internal: ABSENT — available. See above.
  Interpersonal: WEAK — present in form (multiple characters in a room) but not in substance.
  Societal: LATENT — the council itself is a societal structure; Aldric's authority within it is present as background. Not the primary weakness.
  Factional: PARTIAL — the political situation is in the room but not under pressure.
  Existential: ABSENT — not applicable to this scene.

Suggestions:
  1. Give Aldric one moment of internal conflict around the decision to wait. Not a soliloquy — a single beat: he makes the decision to wait, and then notices, without naming it, that the decision felt like a relief rather than a strategy. That beat — small, undeveloped, immediately suppressed — is his self-deception doing work. It costs one sentence in the existing scene and transforms the decision from a non-event into a character revelation.

  2. Give one councillor a position that costs Aldric something to dismiss. The councillor does not need to be right. But if Aldric dismisses a view that some part of him knows is correct — or if one councillor's read of Caradhen is more accurate than Aldric's and the reader can see it even if Aldric cannot — the scene has interpersonal weight. This requires knowing what the councillors' actual positions are, which the draft currently omits.
```
