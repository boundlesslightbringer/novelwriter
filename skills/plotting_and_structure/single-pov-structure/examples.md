## Example

### Input shape

```
Requested POV: Aldric (the king).
User's stated reason: "I want to show what Aldric is thinking after authorising the contingency — before Vara's report reaches him."
Module 2.2 excerpt (last 6 chapters): [as in Multiple POV Structure example — Aldric's last chapter was Ch. 13; current chapter is 14]
Module 2.1 excerpt: [simultaneous events — Vara and Caradhen's meeting at the passes; Vara's courier en route]
```

### Output shape

```
Requested assignment: Aldric (the king).

Validation status: CLEAR.
Validation notes: Aldric's last chapter was Ch. 13; the current chapter is 14. Aldric is in the capital, where he was last placed — no travel or location contradiction. His storyline (the contingency authorisation) has accumulated pressure; a chapter now is structurally well-timed. The assignment is consistent with the story state.

Intent alignment check:
  User's intent: Show Aldric's internal state after authorising the contingency, before Vara's report arrives.
  Story-state confirmation: This intent is achievable. Vara's courier departed the passes on Day 45; it is currently Day 47; the courier is not expected until Day 52. Aldric has not yet received any report. The chapter can show him in the aftermath of the authorisation, in the knowledge gap before the assessment lands — which is structurally the most interesting version of his current state.
  One note: The "contingency" (Ch. 13) was shown only as an authorisation — what Aldric actually instructed the third party to do has not been rendered. A chapter from his POV now is the natural moment to establish, in his own internal terms, what he has set in motion and what he believes it will accomplish. This may be what the user is anticipating, and it is the chapter's most load-bearing potential.

Simultaneous events to account for:
  Vara + Caradhen meeting at the passes (Ch. 14, Day 46–47): Occurring simultaneously. Aldric does not and cannot know the content of this meeting — no communication has reached him. The reader, however, knows it. This is active dramatic irony: the reader is in both heads at once. Aldric's chapter should not give him knowledge of the meeting's content. If he refers to "the envoy's mission" it should be from the general knowledge that she is there, not from any specific awareness of what is happening in the room.
  Vara's courier (en route, Day 45 departure): Aldric does not know the courier has departed — courier dispatch is not the kind of event that would have been communicated to him in advance. No contradiction risk.

Rotation pressure flags:
  Vara — Medium/High: Her assessment brief is building; she is at the passes. Skipping her for one chapter is sound — her pressure is high but not urgent enough to override the user's preference. Her next chapter should follow Aldric's.
  Caradhen — Low: His cliffhanger is best left unresolved for at least one more chapter. Aldric's chapter serves this well.
  Sethric — Low: Dormant thread.

Cliffhanger handling: The previous chapter (Ch. 14, Caradhen) ended on a cliffhanger — Vara asked a question Caradhen cannot easily answer. Cutting to Aldric is a motivated cut: the reader is left in Caradhen's suspended moment, while the chapter reveals what Aldric has authorised. When the reader returns to Caradhen (or to Vara), the dramatic irony will be compounded — the reader now knows both what Vara is assessing and what Aldric is prepared to do about the result. The cut is purposeful.

POV proliferation check: Aldric is an established POV character (Ch. 5–6, Ch. 13). No proliferation concern.
```
