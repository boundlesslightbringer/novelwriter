  Vara: Ch. 1–4 (capital), Ch. 10–12, [next pending] (passes). Currently at the passes.
  Aldric: Ch. 5–6, Ch. 13 (capital). Gap of 7 chapters between appearances. Accumulating narrative pressure.

  Ch. 10 (Vara) and Ch. 11 (Caradhen) — both show the first assessment meeting.
    First rendering (Ch. 10, Vara): Established that Vara is assessing more than negotiating; that Caradhen's non-commitment is a pattern she is cataloguing; that she has begun composing her brief.
    Second rendering (Ch. 11, Caradhen): Shows the same meeting from Caradhen's perspective.
    New information test: PASS (marginal) — Caradhen's internal state (his read of Vara, his unease about her restraint) is not available from Vara's POV. His perspective does add something.
    New register test: PASS — the meeting as professional assessment (Vara) vs. the meeting as an unwanted encounter with someone who might see through him (Caradhen) is a genuine register difference.
    VERDICT: Not redundant. But note: this dual-rendering establishes a pattern. If the second assessment meeting (Ch. 12 and Ch. 14) is also shown from both POVs, the second dual-rendering should be audited more strictly — the reader will already hold the register difference, and a second dual-rendering risks diminishing returns.
    RECOMMENDATION: Monitor. If Ch. 15 shows Vara's version of the Ch. 14 cliffhanger moment, apply the tests again with the bar raised.

Current trajectory: Vara's next chapter should show her composing/completing the assessment; Aldric needs a chapter before the courier arrives so the reader knows what he has authorised before Vara's assessment lands.   Vara–Caradhen (direct interaction, Ch. 10–14): High tension. The reader is inside both heads in alternating chapters; both are managing information; the dramatic irony (the reader knows Vara's assessment brief exists; Caradhen does not) is active and charged.

His storyline is not yet in thematic rhyme with the passes storyline — the reader holds his existence but is not yet in productive irony about it relative to what is happening at the passes.   IMMEDIATE: Aldric needs a chapter within the next 2 chapters. The contingency authorisation is the story's most significant live dramatic irony, and it is at risk of decaying — if the reader spends too long with Vara and Caradhen without returning to Aldric, the contingency will feel like a background fact rather than a ticking structural element.
  
  NEAR-TERM: Develop the Vara assessment → Aldric reception sequence carefully. This is the braid's first major three-way convergence (Aldric receives Vara's assessment of Caradhen; both Caradhen and Vara are now implicated in Aldric's response). The dramatic irony architecture requires that the reader know Aldric's contingency before Vara's assessment lands.
  
  MONITOR: Sethric/Thread 007 activation window. Caradhen's passes chapters are building toward a psychological state that makes the Sethric thread imminent. Watch for a natural entry point.
