The copper-taste of the Gilded Ribcage was always strongest just before a raid. Vane wiped a smear of hydraulic ichor from his brow, his fingers trailing through the hearth-ash he’d rubbed into his neck to mask the heat of his pulse. Beside him in the cramped darkness of the ventilation shaft, Reeve’s breathing was a rhythmic, wet rattle—the 'vent-lung' tax every child of the Warrens paid for the crime of existing.

‘Focus, Vane,’ Reeve hissed, the sound barely carrying over the distant, low thrum of the dormant Aegis. ‘The Gentry are dreaming of silk while the city eats its own leather. Don’t let the hunger make you sloppy.’

Vane didn’t answer. He was staring at the brass-and-sinew conduit pulsing weakly against the stone wall. To the Remnant Peerage, these were sacred relics, the divine veins of a sleeping god. To Vane, they were just another set of pipes to be bled. He reached out, his hand trembling slightly from a week of half-rations, and pressed his palm against the cold metal casing of the secondary grain-vault lock.

He felt it then—a strange, predatory hum that seemed to vibrate not against his skin, but deep within his marrow. It was the inheritance he never asked for: a world built on a biological lock he spent every waking hour trying to pick. The Iron Union was at the gates, their steam-walkers grinding the forests into soot to 'solve' the city’s messiness, but the real rot was here, in the blood. 

‘If we take the central bins, we can feed the Fourth District for a month,’ Vane whispered, his voice raspy. ‘But if we trigger the bio-sensors, the "Needle-Flies" won’t care that we’re starving. They only recognize the code.’

‘Then we break the code,’ Reeve said, his eyes hard in the gloom. ‘Legitimacy is a lie told by people with full bellies. The only thing that's real is who survives the night.’

Vane pulled his toolkit from his belt, the tools worn and notched. He began to strip the casing, his mind a map of the city’s parasitic architecture. He hated this city, hated the way it clung to the basin like a gilded leech, and yet, as his fingers brushed the cooling filaments, a terrifying thought flared: he knew exactly how the machine wanted to be touched. It was a familiarity that felt like a betrayal.
